Sizing a 100 kWh/day hybrid solar system

A training design case during my internship at a solar EPC company in Nairobi: specify the hardware, prove it works hour by hour, and turn it into a business case a client can act on.

The four design decisions

ComponentChoiceWhy
Panel400 W monocrystallineA sweet spot for commercial installations: low cost and high output per panel.
System voltage400 V DC battery busHigher voltage means lower current, so less power is lost in the cables.
BatteryLiFePO4 over lead-acidDeeper depth of discharge and over 6,000 cycles. More expensive upfront, but longer life and near-zero maintenance.
InverterHybrid string invertersThe design must charge and discharge a battery, and at 100 kWh/day a central inverter adds complexity for no benefit.

Proving it with an hourly simulation

I built a 7-day energy balance in Excel, tracking generation, load and battery state of charge hour by hour. My first design wasted over 60% of the solar energy, because the battery sat full for most of the day and the extra generation had nowhere to go. Re-sizing the battery and array against the hourly profile brought waste down to 8%.

Turning engineering into a business case

A client mostly asks three things: what will it cost, when will I break even, and what will I get back over its life. I costed three scenarios, separating CAPEX from OPEX, and worked out annual savings and the payback period for each, so the client could compare the trade-offs and choose.

“Good business cases don’t tell the client what to choose, they show them the trade-offs and let them decide.” From my internship report.

Working with real plants

Performance data

I analysed 28 days of operating data from a commercial solar plant to calculate its performance ratio, and presented the findings to the managers.

Site inspections

I inspected three industrial sites: two pharmaceutical plants that run both solar and steam boilers, and a solar-only factory. On the rooftop system I traced the power path from the panels and MC4 connectors to three inverters fed by 20 strings, then through the combiner and breakers, current transformers, a zero-feed-in controller and the grid transformer. On the boiler side I followed the feedwater pump, furnace, air preheater, dust collector, water softening and pressure-reducing valves.
