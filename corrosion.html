Salt corrosion in rooftop solar mounts

Why Boston’s coastal air is so hard on the aluminium and steel that hold rooftop solar panels in place, and which materials and treatments hold up best.

CourseENG ME 306, Boston University
Team4 students
DateSpring 2026
TopicsCorrosion, processing, materials selection
Sheet6 of 6

The problem

Three chloride and moisture sources hit Boston rooftops together:

  • Marine aerosols: sea spray carries salt that dissolves in humidity and attacks the protective oxide films.
  • Deicing salts: winter road salt adds a second chloride source, concentrating as water evaporates.
  • Freeze–thaw: water expands in joints, widening gaps and loosening fasteners.

Corroded fasteners lose clamping force, so panels can detach in storms. Corroded grounding connections raise resistance and create shock and fire risks. Early failure also cuts system life and wipes out the carbon savings.

The materials science

  • Chloride ions break through the passive Al2O3 film at defects and start pitting.
  • Aluminium rail plus steel fastener plus salt water forms a galvanic cell, and the aluminium dissolves at the bolt.
  • Pits concentrate stress and start fatigue cracks under cyclic wind loads.
  • Processing matters: extrusion elongates the grains and creates paths for intergranular corrosion; a slow quench in T6 heat treatment leaves precipitate-free zones at grain boundaries; sealed anodising builds a dense oxide barrier.

Two solutions compared

Solution 1Solution 2
RailsAnodised aluminiumHot-dip galvanised steel
Fasteners316 stainless steelGalvanised steel
Extra protectionPolymer isolation washersPowder coating
StrengthSalt resistance, light roof loadingSnow load, higher stiffness and tensile strength

The isolation washers in Solution 1 tackle the galvanic couple directly, by breaking the electrical contact between the aluminium and the steel.
